Last Tuesday afternoon, I sat at my desk staring at a blank LinkedIn profile section for twenty minutes. The cursor blinked. I had just received a connection request from a potential buyer in Germany, and before accepting, I wanted to make sure my own summary did not look like a robot wrote it. I opened three browser tabs with templates, copied a few lines, deleted them, and ended up writing nothing. That was the moment I realized my English self introduction problem was not about vocabulary. It was about not knowing what to leave out.

## The email signature mistake I kept making for months

I used to think a long email signature made me look professional. I listed my full name, three phone numbers, two email addresses, company address, website, a quote about quality, and a sentence saying we specialized in seven different product categories. A client in Poland replied once with just one line: "Which product do you actually handle?" That stung. I looked at my own signature and saw the problem. It was a wall of text. Nobody reads a signature that long. They skim it and remember nothing.

Now my email signature is four lines. Name on the first line. Job title and department on the second. Direct phone number with country code on the third. Company website on the fourth. No quotes, no mission statements, no list of seven product ranges. If someone wants to know what I sell, they can click the website or ask me. The signature should answer "who is this and how do I reach them directly," not "here is everything about my company."

## LinkedIn summary that actually sounds like a person

My first LinkedIn summary read like a brochure. I wrote "dynamic and results-oriented international trade professional with extensive experience in global sourcing and supply chain management." A friend who works in marketing read it and laughed. She said it sounded like I was applying for a job I did not want. She was right. That sentence could describe ten thousand people. It said nothing about what I do every day.

I rewrote it by answering one question: what would I tell a stranger at a trade show in thirty seconds? I started with a sentence about what I help buyers with. Something like: "I help overseas buyers find reliable suppliers for home goods and manage orders from sample to shipment." Then two sentences about how I work. Then one sentence about who I usually work with. That is it. No adjectives like "passionate" or "dedicated." No claims about being the best. Just plain statements someone can verify by talking to me.

The hardest part was deleting the sentence about how many years of experience I have. I realized that number means nothing if the reader does not know what those years produced. A buyer does not care that I have been in this industry for eight years. They care whether I can solve their problem this week. So I cut it.

## What I do when I am not sure what to write

When I get stuck, I open a blank document and write down three questions. What does the person reading this need to know first? What do I want them to do after reading it? What would make me sound like a human instead of a template? Then I write one sentence for each answer. That usually gives me a rough draft in under ten minutes. It is not elegant, but it is honest.

Last week I updated my LinkedIn summary again. This time it took five minutes. I did not open any templates. I just wrote what I would say if someone asked me what I do. The German buyer accepted my connection request an hour later and sent a short message asking about our minimum order quantity for kitchenware. No compliments on my profile, just a real question. That felt better than any number of profile views.
